Korea's two high-speed rail operators, Korail (KTX) and SR (SRT), officially merged after South Korea's Fair Trade Commission approved the deal on August 3, 2026 — separated systems, apps and loyalty programs are being folded into one just as Chuseok booking season begins. Integrated trains start running under a single KTX brand from September 1, and the new combined app, Korail+, is now the main way to book. If you're planning to travel by train during Chuseok (September 24–27), the booking dates are already confirmed: priority booking opens September 3, general booking runs September 7–11.

## What the KTX-SRT merger actually changed

South Korea split its high-speed rail into two competing operators in 2016, when SR launched SRT service on the Suseo–Busan line. After nearly a decade of separate booking systems, apps and membership programs, the government reversed course: the Fair Trade Commission cleared Korail's acquisition of SR's rail business on August 3, 2026.

- **One brand.** The SRT name is being retired — from September, all high-speed trains operate under the KTX brand.
- **One app.** The old SRT app and KorailTalk have merged into **Korail+**, which is now the main booking channel for both former KTX and SRT routes.
- **One membership.** Former Korail members are converted automatically. If you only had an SRT account, you need to convert it to an integrated membership before you can book — do this before Chuseok booking opens, not on the day.
- **More seats, lower fares (phased in).** The operator has committed to cutting KTX fares by about 10% over the next three years to match the previously cheaper SRT pricing, and to adding roughly 15,000 weekday and 17,000 weekend seats network-wide. These are commitments for the transition period, not a price cut that has already happened — check the fare shown at booking rather than assuming the discount is live.

## Booking as a foreign traveler

Korail+ supports multiple languages, which makes it usable without fluent Korean — a real improvement over juggling two separate apps and two separate accounts under the old KTX/SRT split.

1. **Set up an account before booking day.** Register on Korail+ (or convert an old SRT-only account) and register a payment method in advance — do not try to do this in the few minutes before booking opens.
2. **Know your ticket limits.** Each person can book up to 12 tickets total for the holiday period, with a maximum of 6 in a single transaction.
3. **Have your route and backup routes ready.** Popular lines toward Busan, Daegu and the southeast sell out within minutes of booking opening. Note a second-choice train and even a second-choice arrival station.
4. **Check both directions separately.** If you're also returning after the holiday, that return-leg booking opens and closes on its own schedule — don't book only the outbound leg.

## If you can't get a train ticket

Missing the booking window isn't the end of your trip. Trains are the fastest option, but not the only one.

- **Watch for cancellations.** Seats released when unpaid priority or general bookings are cancelled around the payment deadlines (September 12–18) are often the best second chance.
- **Intercity buses** run the same routes with more available seats, if a slightly longer trip is acceptable.
- **Domestic flights** (Gimpo–Busan, Gimpo–Jeju) add holiday capacity and are worth comparing if you're travelling with luggage or on a tight schedule.
- **Reconsider your base.** Basing yourself in Seoul rather than chasing a seat to a regional city avoids the booking scramble entirely — see our [Asian Games 2026 schedule guide](/posts/asian-games-2026-schedule-guide/) for what else is happening in the country that month if you want to build a longer trip around it.

## Why this merger happened now

The split rail system created a decade of duplicated infrastructure, competing loyalty programs, and passenger confusion over which app to use for which route. A trial phase began on February 25, 2026, with KTX and SRT trains cross-operating on each other's routes between Seoul/Suseo and Busan, before the full merger received regulatory approval in August. Chuseok 2026 is effectively the first major holiday travel season under the unified system, so some rough edges in the new booking flow are possible — build in extra time when you book.
