The 2026 Asian Games opened September 19 in Aichi-Nagoya, and by the end of the first full day of competition, Team Korea sat third in the medal table with 4 gold, 3 silver and 7 bronze medals — behind China (11 gold) and host nation Japan (6 gold). The headline result was a 67-57 win over Japan in the men's basketball final, Korea's first Asian Games basketball title since 2014. Modern pentathlon supplied Korea's first two golds of the Games. Here's what happened on day one and what's on Team Korea's schedule next.

## Opening ceremony: "Imagine One Asia"

The Games opened on September 19 at Paloma Mizuho Stadium in Nagoya under the slogan "Imagine One Asia," Aichi-Nagoya 2026's official theme. IOC president Kirsty Coventry and Olympic Council of Asia president Sheikh Joaan bin Hamad Al Thani attended, alongside Aichi governor Hideaki Omura, who chairs the organizing committee. Emperor Naruhito and Empress Masako delivered the formal opening address. South Korea's government delegation was led by Culture, Sports and Tourism Minister Choi Hwi-young.

A handful of team-sport preliminaries — including basketball, baseball and football group games — had already been underway for several days before the ceremony, so medal competition was well past its first results by the time the flame was lit.

## Korea's first gold: modern pentathlon

Korea's first gold of the Games came in modern pentathlon, where **Seong Seung-min** won the women's individual event. The men's team followed with a second gold, won by **Jeon Woong-tae**, **Seo Chang-wan** and **Lee Jong-hyun**. Modern pentathlon has not traditionally been one of Korea's strongest events at past Asian Games, making the early one-two finish a genuine surprise start to the medal count.

## Basketball: Korea beats Japan for gold

Men's basketball delivered the biggest result of the opening days. Korea beat host nation Japan 67-57 in the final at Aichi International Arena on September 20 — the first time an Asian Games men's basketball final has been an all-Korea-Japan matchup. Forward **Lee Hyun-jung** led the way with 27 points, 18 rebounds, five three-pointers, three assists and two steals. Under Latvian head coach Mazurs, the team known in Korean media as the "Mazurs squad" claimed Korea's first men's basketball gold since Incheon 2014 and its fifth overall, continuing a pattern of titles roughly every 12 years (2002 Busan, 2014 Incheon, 2026 Nagoya).

## What's next: September 21 and beyond

Team Korea targets more medals on September 21 in swimming and shooting, while three marquee team-sport matchups are scheduled the same day: baseball against Taiwan, women's football against North Korea, and women's volleyball against Japan. Archery — historically Korea's most reliable gold source — doesn't begin its finals window until late September, so the medal table is likely to shift considerably as the Games progress through early October.
