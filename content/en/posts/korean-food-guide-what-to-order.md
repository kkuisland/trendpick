A first Korean meal can feel confusing even when there's an English menu. The short version: **grilled meat (gui), stews (jjigae), rice dishes (bap) and noodles (myeon)** cover much of what you'll see; side dishes (banchan) and water come at no extra charge; you don't tip; and plenty of staples contain no chilli at all. This guide covers what to order, what to expect on the table, and the handful of dishes worth going out of your way for.

## The dishes worth knowing

| Dish | What it is | Spicy? |
|---|---|---|
| Samgyeopsal (삼겹살) | Grilled pork belly, usually cooked at your table | No |
| Bulgogi (불고기) | Thinly sliced beef in a soy-based marinade, grilled or pan-cooked | No |
| Bibimbap (비빔밥) | Warm rice topped with vegetables and gochujang (red chilli paste), mixed together | Mild, adjustable |
| Kimchi jjigae (김치찌개) | Stew made with kimchi | Yes |
| Sundubu jjigae (순두부찌개) | Stew of soft, silky unpressed tofu | Usually |
| Tteokbokki (떡볶이) | Chewy rice cakes, today mostly in gochujang sauce; the older royal-court version is soy-based | Usually |
| Naengmyeon (냉면) | Thin buckwheat noodles, either in cold broth (mul-naengmyeon) or mixed with chilli sauce (bibim-naengmyeon) | Broth: no. Bibim: yes |
| Samgyetang (삼계탕) | Small whole chicken stuffed with glutinous rice, ginseng, jujube and garlic, in broth | No |
| Jjajangmyeon (짜장면) | Noodles in black bean sauce — a Korean-Chinese dish that took off in Incheon | No |
| Korean fried chicken | Fried chicken, plain or sauced | Both exist |

If you only have a few meals: **samgyeopsal** for the experience, **kimchi jjigae** for the flavour of everyday Korean cooking, and **naengmyeon** or **samgyetang** if it's summer — both are classic hot-weather dishes, and many specialist restaurants serve them all year.

## Is Korean food actually spicy?

Less uniformly than its reputation suggests. Korean heat comes mostly from **gochugaru** (chilli flakes) and **gochujang** (fermented chilli paste). Plenty of staples — bulgogi, samgyetang, jjajangmyeon, mul-naengmyeon, most grilled meat — contain no chilli at all.

Useful phrase: **"안 맵게 해주세요"** (an maepge haejuseyo) — "please make it not spicy." A dish whose sauce *is* chilli paste can't really be made mild, so pick a non-spicy dish from the table above instead.

## The free side dishes are not a mistake

Small plates called **banchan (반찬)** arrive with your food — kimchi, seasoned vegetables, braised dishes, whatever the kitchen made that day. The Korea Tourism Organization notes that **asking for extra water or side dishes doesn't cost anything**. Raise a hand and ask for more of the one you liked: **"반찬 더 주세요"** (banchan deo juseyo). Leaving them untouched is completely fine too.

More and more restaurants are self-service: you fetch your own water and refill side dishes from a station, and some use ordering machines instead of a server.

## How Korean BBQ actually works

At a grill restaurant you cook the meat yourself at your table — this trips up a lot of first-timers.

1. **Order by the serving.** Menus price meat per serving, written **1인분** (il-inbun, "one person's portion"). Check the menu for any minimum order before you sit down.
2. **Let the staff help.** If a server comes over to cut or turn the meat, let them.
3. **Wait for the char.** Pork belly needs to be cooked through; don't rush it off the grill.
4. **Build a ssam.** Take a lettuce leaf, add meat, a dab of **ssamjang** (seasoned soybean paste), garlic or grilled kimchi, wrap it and eat it.
5. **Ask about fried rice.** Some places will fry rice (볶음밥, bokkeumbap) in the pan at the end — ask whether they do.

## Table manners that matter

- **Elders first**: start eating once the eldest person at the table has started.
- **Two hands**: give and receive things with both hands. When someone older pours you a drink, hold your glass with both hands.
- **Pouring drinks**: don't pour your own drink — let someone else pour for you, and pour for anyone more senior first.
- **Chopsticks and spoon**: don't leave them sticking up in a bowl of rice; Koreans use the spoon for rice and soup. Put them back where they were when you finish.
- **Shared dishes**: stews and side dishes are shared from the centre of the table — don't dig through them for the best pieces. If you'd rather have your own portion, ask for a small plate: **"앞접시 주세요"** (apjeopsi juseyo).
- **Tipping**: not a Korean custom and not expected. Some major hotels and upscale restaurants add a 10 percent service charge to the bill instead.

## Ordering without Korean

Three phrases carry you a long way:

- **"이거 주세요"** (igeo juseyo) — "this one, please" (while pointing at the menu)
- **"계산해 주세요"** (gyesanhae juseyo) — "the bill, please"
- **"물 주세요"** (mul juseyo) — "water, please" — though at self-service places you fetch it yourself

The bill is often settled at the counter on your way out rather than at the table.

## Convenience store food is genuinely good

Korean convenience stores (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven, emart24) are a legitimate meal option, not a last resort. Samgak gimbap (triangle kimbap) — a rice triangle with a filling, wrapped in seaweed — is one of their steadiest sellers, and each chain carries its own cup ramyeon, baked or smoked eggs, and products made in collaboration with brands and restaurants. Line-ups change often, so browse rather than hunt for one specific item.

::faq
Q: What should I eat on my first day in Korea?
A: Korean BBQ (samgyeopsal) for the full experience, or kimchi jjigae for the flavour of everyday Korean cooking. Both are widely available and hard to get wrong.

Q: Is all Korean food spicy?
A: No. Bulgogi, samgyetang, jjajangmyeon, mul-naengmyeon and most grilled meats contain no chilli. The heat comes from chilli flakes (gochugaru) and chilli paste (gochujang), so dishes with a red sauce are the ones to watch.

Q: Are the side dishes free?
A: Yes. The Korea Tourism Organization notes that asking for extra side dishes or water doesn't cost anything. At self-service restaurants you refill them yourself.

Q: Do I tip in Korean restaurants?
A: No. Tipping is not a Korean custom and isn't expected. Some major hotels and upscale restaurants add a 10 percent service charge to the bill instead.

Q: Can vegetarians eat well in Korea?
A: It takes some planning. Korean food is often cooked with meat or fish broth, and kimchi seasoning often contains salted seafood or fish sauce. Temple food — made without animal ingredients — is the most reliable option, and bibimbap can be ordered without meat and egg if you ask.

Q: What is the one dish tourists most often miss?
A: Cold noodles — naengmyeon, especially the mul (broth) version. It's a classic summer dish, and specialist restaurants serve it year-round.
