Team Korea's strongest medal chances at the 2026 Asian Games run through archery, badminton and swimming, with baseball and football drawing the biggest domestic attention because both carry mandatory military service exemption for gold. The men's recurve archery team includes Kim Woo-jin, Kim Je-deok and Lee Woo-seok — but not triple Olympic and Asian Games gold medalist Lim Si-hyeon, who missed the women's team at trials. Badminton's An Se-young is defending her Hangzhou 2022 singles title against a stacked all-Asian field, and swimmer Hwang Sun-woo heads a young roster racing at a venue outside Aichi-Nagoya entirely. Here's who and what to watch, sport by sport.

## Archery: Korea's deepest bench, and a genuine shock

Korea treats Olympic and Asian medals as almost a formality in archery, and the men's recurve side keeps that streak alive: **Kim Woo-jin**, **Kim Je-deok** and **Lee Woo-seok** made the national team for Aichi-Nagoya. What's making headlines instead is who didn't. **Lim Si-hyeon** — a triple gold medalist at both the Paris 2024 Olympics and the Hangzhou 2022 Asian Games — finished 10th at the March 2026 national selection trials, outside the top 8 that qualify for the team, and will not compete this September.

Korea's archery selection is purely merit-based: there's no reigning-champion exemption, so even a world-beater has to requalify every cycle. It's the clearest illustration of how deep the country's talent pool is, and it means the women's recurve team heading to Aichi-Nagoya is a genuinely different lineup than the one that swept Paris and Hangzhou.

## Swimming: Hwang Sun-woo leads a race held outside Nagoya

**Hwang Sun-woo**, Korea's world champion freestyler with six Asian Games medals from Hangzhou (two of them gold), and Olympic medalist **Kim Woo-min** are both racing in Aichi-Nagoya's swimming program, which runs roughly September 20–25. One detail worth knowing before you plan a trip around it: swimming isn't held in Aichi or Nagoya at all. It's staged at the **Tokyo Aquatics Centre**, reused from the Tokyo 2020 Olympics, so fans hoping to combine a swimming session with the opening ceremony need to budget travel time between Tokyo and Nagoya.

## Baseball: pro players, a real group stage, and exemption on the line

Baseball at the Asian Games uses active KBO professionals rather than an amateur or development squad, which is part of why it draws such heavy attention in Korea: a gold medal comes with a mandatory military service exemption, so every roster spot matters. Korea has been drawn into a group with Taiwan, Hong Kong and Thailand for the preliminary round, reported to run **September 21–23**, with a rest day on the 24th before the Super Round on the 25th–26th and the medal games on **September 27**. Taiwan is historically Korea's toughest test in the group stage; Japan, as co-hosts, are the team most likely to meet Korea deeper in the bracket.

## Football: defending champions, minus their biggest name

Korea's men's team enters as the **defending gold medalist** from Hangzhou 2022, competing as an U-23 squad with three overage "wildcard" additions, as the men's competition allows. This cycle's wildcards are **Yang Hyun-jun** (Celtic), **Um Ji-sung** (Swansea City) and **Lee Ki-hyuk** (Gangwon FC) — Europe-based **Lee Kang-in**, already exempt from military service through the 2022 gold, was not part of the call-up. The squad is drawn largely from the group that competed at the 2026 AFC U-23 Asian Cup earlier this year, where Korea finished fourth, an underwhelming result the team will want to answer for in Japan.

## Badminton: An Se-young defends against an all-Asian top 10

Every player currently ranked in the world's top 10 in women's singles is Asian, which makes **An Se-young's** title defense one of the most competitive individual events of the Games. She arrives fresh off winning both the 2026 BWF World Championships and the BWF Asia Championships, but China's Wang Zhiyi and Chen Yufei, plus Japan's Akane Yamaguchi playing in front of a home region crowd, all stand between her and a repeat gold. Korea's badminton events run within the broader September 20–29 window.

## What's still unconfirmed
