South Korea's government announced a package of Chuseok 2026 support measures on September 1, and two parts of it directly affect anyone traveling in the country that week. Highway tolls nationwide are free for four days, September 24–27, and KTX fares are already about 10% cheaper — not a future promise, but a price cut that took effect the same day the plan was announced. There's also a round of accommodation discount vouchers for stays outside Seoul. Here's what each one actually means if you're on the road or the rails during the holiday.

## Free highway tolls: September 24–27

Every expressway toll in the country is waived for the four-day stretch of the Chuseok holiday, September 24 through 27, 2026. This is an annual measure the government runs for both of Korea's big holidays (Chuseok and Lunar New Year), and it applies automatically — there's no pass to register or app to activate. Any vehicle on a tolled expressway during that window pays nothing, including trips that start just before the window opens or finish just after it closes.

For visitors, this matters most if you're renting a car for a countryside trip — to see relatives-in-law, visit a folk village, or just get out of Seoul. It won't help with parking or with city-center congestion charges, only expressway tolls.

## KTX fares are already 10% cheaper

This is the part worth flagging clearly: KTX fares dropped by an average of 10% starting September 1, 2026, as part of the completed merger between Korail (KTX) and SR (SRT) into a single operator. Some earlier coverage described the fare cut as a multi-year commitment being phased in — as of September 1, it's live and already reflected in booking prices, not something still pending.

| Route | Old fare | New fare (from Sept 1) |
|---|---|---|
| Seoul → Busan | ₩59,800 | ₩54,400 |
| Yongsan → Gwangju-Songjeong | ₩46,800 | ₩42,000 |

Seat supply also increased alongside the price cut — roughly 15,000 more seats on weekdays and up to 17,000 more on weekends, network-wide, now that Suseo-departing trains and Seoul Station-departing trains are booked through one system. If you're booking Chuseok travel, check the fare shown in the **Korail+** app rather than assuming the old price — it should already reflect the lower fare. For the full holiday booking calendar (priority booking, general booking windows, payment deadlines), see our [KTX-SRT merger and Chuseok booking guide](/posts/korea-ktx-srt-merger-chuseok-booking/).

## Accommodation vouchers for stays outside Seoul

The government released 80,000 discount vouchers for accommodation booked outside the greater Seoul area, aimed at pulling some of the holiday travel demand toward regional cities rather than concentrating it in the capital. Some rural destinations are also offering a partial refund, in local gift certificates, on money spent during a visit. These programs typically run through Korean travel booking platforms and may require a Korean phone number or ID to redeem — worth checking eligibility on the platform before counting on it, rather than assuming it applies the same way a hotel discount code would abroad.

If a regional stay isn't in the cards, Seoul itself is often the calmer option during Chuseok: the city empties out as residents head to their hometowns, hotel availability loosens up, and you can skip the holiday traffic on the expressways entirely.

## Onnuri gift certificates: mostly for local shoppers

The other headline piece of the plan — a higher purchase limit and discount on digital **Onnuri** gift certificates, usable at traditional markets, from September 16–20 — is aimed at domestic consumers and generally requires a Korean bank account to buy. It's useful context for understanding why markets are pushing extra promotions that week, but it's not something a short-term visitor typically taps into directly.

## What this means if you're visiting Korea for Chuseok

- **Renting a car for a countryside day trip** is meaningfully cheaper this year — the toll-free window covers exactly the days most people are on the road anyway.
- **Booking a KTX ticket**, the fare itself is lower than it would have been before September, on top of whatever booking-date strategy you're using.
- **Choosing where to stay**, regional accommodation may carry an extra discount if you book through a platform that supports the voucher — but confirm eligibility rather than assuming it applies.
- **Traffic is still traffic.** Free tolls don't mean an empty road — the first and last days of the holiday remain the most congested days of the year on Korea's expressways.

Q: Are highway tolls really free for the whole Chuseok holiday?
A: Yes, for the four-day window of September 24–27, 2026. It applies automatically to any vehicle on a tolled expressway during that period, with no registration needed.

Q: Is the 10% KTX fare cut actually in effect, or still a future promise?
A: It's already in effect. The average 10% reduction took effect September 1, 2026, alongside the completed merger of KTX and SRT into a single operator — it should already be reflected in the fare shown when you book.

Q: Can foreign tourists use the accommodation discount vouchers?
A: Not necessarily. The vouchers are distributed through Korean travel booking platforms and may require a Korean phone number or ID to redeem. Check the specific platform's eligibility rules before counting on the discount.

Q: Does the toll exemption cover city tolls or parking, too?
A: No — it applies only to expressway tolls. City-center congestion charges and parking fees are unaffected.

Q: Will trains be more crowded because fares are cheaper?
A: Possibly, along with the usual holiday demand — Chuseok train travel is already heavily booked well in advance regardless of price. Book as early as the official booking window allows.

Q: Where can I check the exact KTX fare for my route?
A: In the Korail+ app or on the Korail website — search your route and the discounted fare should already be shown for travel from September 1, 2026 onward.
