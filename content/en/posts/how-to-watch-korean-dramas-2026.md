There is no single service that carries every K-drama, and that's the thing worth understanding before you subscribe to anything. Rights are split by broadcaster and by country: **Netflix and Disney+ keep their own originals, Rakuten Viki runs a large Asian-drama catalogue with a free ad-supported tier, and KOCOWA+ specialises in the three big Korean free-to-air networks (KBS, MBC, SBS)**. The practical approach is to pick the show first, check where it's licensed in your country, and then subscribe.

## The main services compared

| Service | Best for | Free tier |
|---|---|---|
| Netflix | Netflix originals, large back catalogue | No — cheapest plan is "Standard with ads" |
| Rakuten Viki | Large Asian-drama catalogue, community subtitles | Yes — selected titles with ads, in SD |
| KOCOWA+ | KBS, MBC and SBS dramas and variety shows | Sold as a paid subscription |
| Disney+ | Its own Korean originals | No |
| Viu | 15 markets in Southeast Asia, the Middle East and South Africa | Yes, ad-supported |
| Tubi | Older Korean titles, at no cost | Yes, free with ads |

On Viki, paying for **Viki Pass Standard** removes ads and gives HD (720p); **Viki Pass Plus** adds Full HD (1080p), more devices at once and offline viewing, according to Viki's help centre.

One change worth knowing: **in December 2025, KOCOWA announced it had ended its content distribution through Viki**, so Viki Pass Plus no longer includes KOCOWA programmes and KOCOWA directs those viewers to its own KOCOWA+ subscription. If you're returning to K-dramas after a gap, re-check where your shows live.

## Which service for which network

Korean dramas air on different broadcasters, and that is a useful starting point when a title is missing:

- **KBS, MBC, SBS** (free-to-air networks) → KOCOWA+ is the specialist. KOCOWA's own company description names KBS, MBC and SBS as its partner broadcasters, and it says new episodes arrive within hours of the Korean broadcast. It added Europe and Oceania in April 2024; check its site for your country.
- **Netflix originals** → Netflix only
- **Disney+ Korean originals** → Disney+ only
- **Coupang Play, TVING and Wavve originals** (Korean domestic streaming services) → look the title up for your country; whether it is licensed abroad differs show by show.

## Free and legal options

You do not have to pay to watch K-dramas legally.

- **Viki's free tier** lets you watch selected titles with ads at standard definition; ad-free HD and Viki's exclusive titles sit behind Viki Pass.
- **Tubi** is a free, ad-supported service with a Korean drama category. Its line-up differs by country, so check the live category page.
- **KBS WORLD TV**, KBS's official international YouTube channel, posts KBS programme content. Check each video's subtitle options before you start.
- **Viu** offers an ad-supported free tier alongside its paid plan. Its parent company PCCW lists 15 markets across Southeast Asia, the Middle East and South Africa; the United States is not one of them.

## How to check availability before subscribing

Licensing changes by country and by date, and articles go stale fast — including this one. The reliable method:

1. Look up the specific drama on **JustWatch** with your country selected.
2. **Confirm on the platform's own page** before paying, since aggregators lag behind rights changes.
3. Check whether it's the full series or only some seasons.

If a site offers new K-dramas free with no ads and no visible broadcaster or licence relationship, treat it as unlicensed and don't enter payment details there.

## A note on subtitles

Viki's subtitles are largely made by its volunteer community, and Viki says titles on the platform are subtitled in more than 150 languages. If you watch in a language other than English, check Viki first. On KOCOWA+, its December 2025 announcement lists subtitles in English, Spanish, Portuguese, Chinese and Vietnamese.

::faq
Q: What is the single best service for K-dramas?
A: There isn't one that covers everything. Netflix and Disney+ hold their own originals, KOCOWA+ is the specialist for KBS, MBC and SBS, and Rakuten Viki has a large catalogue with a free ad-supported tier. Most dedicated viewers end up with two.

Q: Can I watch new episodes the same week they air in Korea?
A: Often yes. KOCOWA+ says new KBS, MBC and SBS episodes arrive within hours of the Korean broadcast. For other shows it depends on the licensing deal for that title in your country.

Q: Are Viki and KOCOWA still connected?
A: No. KOCOWA announced in December 2025 that it had ended its distribution through Viki, and Viki Pass Plus no longer includes KOCOWA content. A title on one service is not necessarily on the other.

Q: Is there a free legal way to watch K-dramas?
A: Yes — Viki's ad-supported free tier (selected titles, SD), Tubi's Korean drama category, Viu's free tier in its markets, and KBS's official KBS WORLD TV YouTube channel.

Q: Why did a drama disappear from my streaming service?
A: Licences expire. Rights are sold for fixed terms and specific territories, so titles rotate off services regularly and may reappear elsewhere.
