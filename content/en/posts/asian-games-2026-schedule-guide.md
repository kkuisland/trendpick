The 20th Asian Games open in Aichi and Nagoya, Japan on **September 19, 2026**, and run for 16 days through **October 4**. The opening ceremony starts at 6 p.m. local time at Paloma Mizuho Stadium, where 45 national teams — Team Korea among them — will compete across 43 sports for roughly 469 gold medals. A handful of team-sport preliminaries actually begin about ten days earlier, so the Games are already underway before the ceremony. If you're planning to watch or attend, here's what's confirmed so far.

## Key dates at a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Overall dates | Sept 19 – Oct 4, 2026 (16 days) |
| Early events | Some team-sport preliminaries start around Sept 10 |
| Opening ceremony | Sept 19, 6–8 p.m. JST, Paloma Mizuho Stadium |
| Closing ceremony | Oct 4, 6–7:30 p.m. JST, same venue |
| Host region | Aichi Prefecture & Nagoya, with some events in Tokyo, Osaka and Shizuoka |
| Scale | 43 sports, 71 disciplines, ~469 gold medals, ~15,000 athletes from 45 nations |

Japan last hosted a Summer Asian Games in Hiroshima in 1994, so this is the country's first time back in 32 years. Because Japan and Korea share the same time zone, Korean viewers can watch every session live without any time-zone math.

## Where the events are held

The Games are spread across roughly 53 venues, but a handful matter most for spectators:

- **Paloma Mizuho Stadium (Nagoya)** — the main stadium; hosts the opening and closing ceremonies plus athletics.
- **IG Arena (Nagoya)** — a newly built indoor venue for judo and basketball.
- **Toyota Stadium (Toyota City)** — the main football venue.
- **Tokyo Aquatics Centre** — swimming and diving, reused from the Tokyo 2020 Olympics.
- **Izu Velodrome (Shizuoka)** — track cycling, also a Tokyo 2020 venue.

Reusing Tokyo 2020 facilities for aquatics and cycling is one reason organizers describe this as a leaner, lower-cost edition of the Games compared with recent hosts.

## Tickets and the free fan zone

General ticket sales opened to everyone on **June 30, 2026**, and are sold first-come, first-served through the official site as long as seats remain for each session. Prices vary widely by sport and seating zone, but the cheapest tickets — Zone C seats for most disciplines — run under ¥3,500, with general admission starting around ¥1,500 for some events. Top-tier seats for marquee finals can run up to roughly ¥30,000.

To get face-value pricing rather than resale rates, register a free "Supporter ID" on the official ticketing site (tickets-aichi-nagoya2026.org) before buying. If you'd rather not buy a ticket at all, a free public fan zone at **Hisaya Odori Park** in central Nagoya runs throughout the Games with big-screen viewing, no ticket required.

A torch relay has been travelling through every municipality in Aichi Prefecture since **August 22**, building up to the opening ceremony — expect road closures and local events along its route if you're in the area.

## Team Korea's delegation

Korea's national Olympic committee confirmed a delegation of **792 athletes across 41 sports** for these Games, according to Korean sports media reporting in late August 2026. The team's official send-off ceremony is set for **September 3**, with the main delegation departing for Nagoya on **September 17**, two days ahead of the opening ceremony.

Because this is a Summer Games without ice or snow sports, Korea's traditional medal strength shifts toward archery, fencing, taekwondo, swimming, badminton and table tennis. Baseball and football also draw heavy attention every cycle, since both send development-focused squads that double as a look at the next generation of pro talent.

## Planning a trip to Nagoya

Nagoya sits roughly two hours from Korea by air, which makes it one of the more accessible Asian Games hosts in recent memory for Korean and other Northeast Asian travelers. A few things worth checking before you book:

- **Flights**: Incheon isn't the only option — Cheongju and Busan (Gimhae) also run Nagoya routes, so compare total cost including domestic travel if you're not near Seoul.
- **Flight + hotel bundles**: During a major event like this, package deals sometimes beat booking flights and hotels separately — worth comparing both.
- **Cancellation terms**: Event schedules and matchups can still shift, so a refundable booking is safer than a locked-in rate this close to the Games.
