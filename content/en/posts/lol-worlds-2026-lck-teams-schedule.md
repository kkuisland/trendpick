The 2026 League of Legends World Championship runs **October 15 to November 14, 2026**, across three U.S. cities — Los Angeles, Allen (Texas), and Brooklyn, New York. All four qualified Korean teams — **Gen.G, Hanwha Life Esports, T1 and Dplus KIA** — skip the Play-In Stage entirely and open the tournament directly in the Swiss Stage. Defending three-time champion T1 arrives as the record holder with six World titles overall, but faces genuine competition from 2026 LCK Summer champion Gen.G and MSI 2026 champion Hanwha Life Esports. Every match streams free on Twitch, YouTube and lolesports.com, so no regional broadcast deal is needed to watch.

## Full schedule: play-in to final

This is the United States' fifth time hosting Worlds, and the first ever held in Texas. The tournament unfolds in four stages over a month, moving from Los Angeles to Allen, Texas, before finishing at Brooklyn's Barclays Center.

| Stage | Dates | Venue |
|---|---|---|
| Play-In Stage | Oct 15–18 | Riot Games Arena, Los Angeles |
| Swiss Stage — Round 1 | Oct 23–26 | Credit Union of Texas Event Center, Allen, TX |
| Swiss Stage — Round 2 | Oct 28–31 | Credit Union of Texas Event Center, Allen, TX |
| Quarterfinals & Semifinals | Nov 3–8 | Credit Union of Texas Event Center, Allen, TX |
| Final | Nov 14 | Barclays Center, Brooklyn, NY |

The Swiss Stage format means every team plays a set number of best-of-one and best-of-three matches against opponents with a similar win-loss record, rather than being locked into a fixed group from the start — so seeding only decides your first-round opponent, not your whole path.

## LCK's four teams and how they qualified

Korea sends four teams to the main event, each getting there a different way:

- **Gen.G** — won the 2026 LCK Summer Season Playoffs, taking the LCK's #1 seed.
- **Hanwha Life Esports** — qualified automatically by winning the 2026 Mid-Season Invitational (MSI).
- **T1** — locked a top-four LCK playoff finish; the reigning three-time World champion.
- **Dplus KIA** — claimed the LCK's fourth and final Worlds berth by beating KT Rolster 3-1 in the lower bracket.

## Why every Korean team skips the Play-In Stage

Normally only two or three LCK teams get a bye straight to the Swiss Stage, with the rest starting in the Play-In. This year all four Korean teams avoid it. Hanwha Life Esports already secured its spot through its MSI 2026 win, so its regular playoff-based seed passed down to the next-ranked LCK team instead of going unused — and that extra seed also came with a direct Swiss Stage bye, lifting every LCK team past the Play-In Stage.

## Is T1 still the team to beat?

T1 enters Worlds 2026 as the most decorated team in the tournament's history, with six championships overall and three in a row after edging fellow LCK side KT Rolster 3-2 in the 2025 final. That streak makes T1 the obvious favorite on paper, but both Gen.G and Hanwha Life Esports arrive on stronger 2026 form — Gen.G as this year's LCK Summer champion and Hanwha Life as the team that beat the rest of the world at MSI. With three of the four LCK seeds capable of winning it, an all-Korean final is a realistic outcome for a fourth straight year.

## Tickets: what's sold out and what's not

General admission tickets for the Brooklyn final went on sale July 24, 2026, with face-value prices between $120 and $350 — and reportedly sold out within about 30 seconds. Resale listings have since run well above face value, with some seats going for over $1,800 and premium listings far higher. Riot Games confirmed a second wave of general admission tickets in August after the initial sale drew complaints, so check the official Worlds ticketing page directly for current availability before assuming everything is gone [confirm before you buy].
